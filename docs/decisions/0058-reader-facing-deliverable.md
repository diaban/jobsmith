## From “Agents (`agents/`) — what an agent *is*”

**The deliverable knows who reads it** (#58). Every step upstream of it writes for the *run* — `research` produces notes, `analysis` findings, and `critique` is agent-facing by design ("challenge the material, suggest improvements" is a memo to whoever will redo the work). No prompt named an audience, so that material came back out with its own shape: a request for a printable one-pager produced a deck whose slides were *Contraintes de livrable*, *État actuel et risques*, *Prochaines étapes* — a status report on the work, handed to someone who asked about a subject and does not know a DAG exists. Four changes, each at the place the register is actually decided. **The two prompts that produce a deliverable name their reader** (`generator_system_prompt` here and in `core/profile.py`, `SlideDeckCapability.DESIGN_SYSTEM`) and rule out what that reader must never be handed: no state of the work, no options to choose between, no template to fill, no request for input. **`critique` is evidence, never voice** — the issue's second decision, answered by keeping it in the material and labelling what it *is*, in both consumers: `CritiqueCapability.HEADING` read "Internal review of the work — not of the subject", and `SlideDeckCapability.MATERIAL` carries a third field saying what each block is. (#82 changed what that field has to say, because the step stopped reviewing the work; what it must not become — the document's voice, a slide of its own — is unchanged.) Removing it instead would leave a step whose only consumer is the provenance table; a review that says a claim is unsupported is worth having before it reaches a slide. **#73 reversed the generator's half of that** — see the bullet below: a label says what a block *is* and does not stop a weak model copying the best-structured thing it can see. **`SUBJECT_ONLY_RULE` (`_step.py`) goes on every prompt that produces material**, because a request carries a subject *and* instructions about the document ("a one-pager, plus a deck for the team") and a step handed both analyses the task — which is how a report about chairs grew a section of advice on building the deck. **`slide_deck.render_context` stopped handing over its outline**: its docstring already said the answer is "not a transcript of the deck", and measured on a real run the answer transcribed the ten slide titles, twice, bringing the deck's register with them. The generator is told the annex exists and given nothing to copy; `render_report` still shows the outline, because that is the provenance section a human reads. Measured, not judged: `report_reader_facing` in `evals/scoring.py` is the one check that reads *wording* and says so — register lives nowhere else — and it is a floor under these prompts, not a proof of good prose.
