# 0228 — Everything is a step: the compiler generates the result slots, the planner fills them, and a step reads the request only as background

- **Issue:** #228 · **PR:** #229 · **Status:** accepted
- **Rule in `CLAUDE.md`:** "**Vocabulary: `docs/glossary.md`**" (Project overview); the rules on programs land with steps 1c and R

**Context.** The strict-mode probe (0219) listed `slide_deck` among the work ops, against the note's own "Primitives v1", and the deck request caused 14 of the 35 failures of its runs: the planner read the whole request as its task, placed the deck alone, and had nothing to reference. Who owns the deliverable was never decided: `document_intent` picks the formats, the writing and the Reporters produce the answer and the files, the planner plans the deck (0061, 0080, 0090, 0126 and 0129 patch that boundary). And every component reads the raw request (0129: `analysis` designed slides, 8 times in 8).

**Decision.** With the owner, written in `docs/design/compiler-v1.md` ("What a program delivers", settled points 6–9) and `docs/glossary.md`: (1) everything is a step; a program has `steps` and a `result` whose slots (answer, deliverables, typed object) the compiler generates; the planner fills each slot's `instruction` and `material`, never whether it exists; strict mode makes every key required, so the grammar guarantees the slots. (2) Every model-calling step gets an instruction written for it; the request is marked background (0004's guard). (3) The framework stays chat-agnostic: a result contract declared by the caller, inferred by the conversation's adapter. (4) An authored deliverable is written from the material, a sibling of the answer. Step 1c ships `result` with one `answer` slot; the contract is step R.

**Alternatives and why not.** The planner writes the mandatory steps, then analysis, repair and a last-resort append: it rewrites known facts, the only room left being for errors. Body and tail (the compiler appends the writing and the deliverables after the planner's steps): the deliverable stays outside the program, so the answer cannot reference it to present it. A reserved `$answer`: a special case for one slot. The deck from the answer: no richer than the answer. PlanCompiler (exporters as ordinary nodes, no sink check, success judged afterwards on files) has an external judge jobsmith lacks; LLMCompiler's joiner keeps the answer out of the plan.

**Consequences.** Open for step R: the default mode of a deck request (today the full answer comes with the deck) and whether an authored deliverable runs beside the answer or after its verdict. The webhook and its payload (#225, #226) and `jobsmith run --format` (#227) are filed. Renames (`Plan` → `Program`, `CapabilitySpec` → `OpSpec`…) ride the steps that touch that code.
